# Candidate Cards

This file is for the edited deck, not the raw mining pool.

A prompt belongs here when it survives editorial comparison with nearby prompts and appears likely to provoke a distinct artistic move.

Selection criteria:

- evocative rather than explanatory;
- operationally distinct from neighbouring cards;
- open enough to work across media;
- capable of changing a decision, not merely describing an attitude;
- no requirement to expose or preserve the conceptual framework that generated it.

The ordering below is provisional and deliberately ungrouped. Source categories do not determine the structure of the deck.

1. Hide it by adding more.
2. Remove the explanation, not the result.
3. Treat an accident as precedent.
4. Give it somewhere it is allowed to exist.
5. Let the weakest element decide what stays.
6. Change only what surrounds it.
7. Reopen something you considered settled.
8. Make one deliberate element look like a mistake.
9. Let yesterday's solution become today's constraint.
10. Introduce decoys that resemble the important thing.
11. Choose what makes the rest more difficult.
12. Rename something and respond to its new name.
13. Keep the correction after the mistake is gone.
14. Force the work to be encountered in the wrong sequence.
15. Let the copy outrank the original.
16. Leave a prepared space permanently unfilled.
17. Make two incompatible readings equally necessary.
18. Notice what you have stopped noticing.
19. Replace it with something that almost fits.
20. Let habit finish the work.
21. Break a connection without removing either part.
22. Make the repair more visible than the damage.
23. Refuse the most obvious solution before starting.
24. Hide the decision inside the procedure.
25. Stop just before the thing you are most curious about.
26. Punish one gesture once.
27. Make the mistake look authoritative.
28. Repeat one element until it becomes background.
29. Undo the rule but keep its consequences.
30. Keep only what passes an irrelevant test.
31. Change its scale until its role changes.
32. Give the secondary element the best position.
33. Work from a memory you know is wrong.
34. Invent a rule but do not state it.
35. Make every element demand equal attention.
36. Remove the most accomplished part.
37. Reverse something too late.
38. Put what matters where attention arrives last.
39. Keep what almost belongs.
40. Correct something until it becomes unstable.
41. Reintroduce what the work has learned to live without.
42. Withhold the element that would resolve the whole.
43. Protect what was meant to be temporary.
44. Make the exception govern the rule.
45. Make the simplest path indirect.
46. Change one default and nothing else.
47. Preserve the trace but lose the event.
48. Make the background responsible for the foreground.
49. Decide what counts as finished, then violate it.
50. Leave evidence of something that never happened.
51. Add a response that changes the question.
52. Reconstruct something from an incomplete record.
53. Make the unwanted effect indispensable.
54. Let it remain, but take away its audience.
55. Make the next move answer an imagined criticism.
56. Treat the interruption as the work.

# Mining Pass 1

Raw artistic operations mined from the censorship taxonomy. These are source material, not finished cards. Redundancy is intentional at this stage.

## Exclusion / prevention

1. Prevent the expected element from entering.
2. Refuse the most obvious solution before starting.
3. Make one material unavailable.
4. Begin with one possible move already forbidden.
5. Leave a prepared space permanently unfilled.
6. Complete the work without its presumed centre.
7. Allow everything except the thing you first wanted.
8. Decide in advance what cannot appear.
9. Make an opening that nothing may enter.
10. Prevent one connection from ever being made.
11. Withhold the element that would resolve the whole.
12. Make absence a condition rather than an accident.

## Exclusion / erasure

13. Remove an element after it has begun to organise the others.
14. Erase the part that proves how the work was made.
15. Destroy one completed component but keep its effects.
16. Remove a connection rather than either endpoint.
17. Delete the thing that makes two parts intelligible together.
18. Take something away and do not repair the gap.
19. Remove the evidence that something was there.
20. Erase selectively enough that the original can still be inferred.
21. Remove the centre and preserve everything that depended on it.
22. Destroy the source but retain the copy.
23. Remove the explanation, not the result.
24. Erase the earlier state from which the present one developed.

## Attenuation / demotion

25. Keep it, but move it somewhere unlikely to be noticed.
26. Make the important thing smaller.
27. Give the secondary element the best position.
28. Allow the strongest thing only at the edge.
29. Reduce its recurrence each time it returns.
30. Preserve its form while diminishing its influence.
31. Let it remain, but take away its audience.
32. Put what matters where attention arrives last.
33. Make the loud thing quiet without changing what it says.
34. Lower its rank without removing it.

## Attenuation / friction and delay

35. Make access require an unnecessary step.
36. Put an obstacle between the viewer and the most direct reading.
37. Require one action before another can be encountered.
38. Delay the expected event past its useful moment.
39. Make the simplest path indirect.
40. Force the work to be encountered in the wrong sequence.
41. Let understanding arrive one step too late.
42. Make something easy to see but difficult to reach.
43. Introduce a small inconvenience and repeat it.
44. Make the shortest route unavailable.
45. Place the answer somewhere it can only be found after it is needed.

## Attenuation / containment

46. Give it somewhere it is allowed to exist.
47. Confine the strongest material to the smallest region.
48. Keep one idea inside a boundary it may not cross.
49. Let an element recur, but only under one condition.
50. Build a margin and send something there.
51. Isolate what would otherwise spread.
52. Give the disruptive element its own room.
53. Permit it completely, but only in one place.
54. Draw a boundary around something after it has begun to escape.

## Transformation / modification

55. Change it without removing it.
56. Alter only what surrounds it.
57. Preserve the structure but change the material.
58. Translate one element into another medium.
59. Remove parts while preserving the grammar.
60. Distort it just enough to remain recognisable.
61. Change its scale until its role changes.
62. Keep the material and change its neighbours.
63. Rename something and respond to its new name.
64. Change the category without changing the object.
65. Change only the title, label or instruction.
66. Preserve the contour and change the interior.
67. Preserve the function and change the appearance.
68. Preserve the appearance and change the function.
69. Change the frame until the original means something else.
70. Alter what counts as foreground.
71. Describe the same thing differently and then believe the description.

## Transformation / substitution

72. Replace it without changing what it does.
73. Put something peripheral in the position of the centre.
74. Copy the structure and replace the content.
75. Produce a substitute, then remove the original.
76. Replace evidence of the process with a cleaner account.
77. Give the replacement every privilege of the original.
78. Replace a difficult element with an easier equivalent.
79. Exchange two elements while preserving their roles.
80. Substitute something that almost fits.
81. Replace what happened with what should have happened.
82. Preserve the gap but change what fills it.

## Saturation / flooding

83. Hide it by adding more.
84. Repeat one element until it becomes background.
85. Add alternatives until choice stops mattering.
86. Surround a precise mark with imprecise echoes.
87. Fill the quietest area with competing events.
88. Make every element demand equal attention.
89. Introduce decoys that resemble the important thing.
90. Add enough context to obscure the point.
91. Explain until the simple relation becomes difficult to see.
92. Repeat the wrong thing more often than the right one.
93. Make the signal indistinguishable from ornament.
94. Increase density rather than alter what is already there.
95. Divert attention immediately before the important event.
96. Put the singular event among many similar events.
97. Keep adding until the hierarchy collapses.
98. Create several plausible readings and let them interfere.

## Responsive restraint

99. Work as though one choice is forbidden.
100. Invent a rule but do not state it.
101. Imagine one material would be rejected; avoid it.
102. Make one early punishment shape every later choice.
103. Let the first mistake create a prohibition for the rest of the work.
104. Continue according to a rule whose origin you have forgotten.
105. Remove something before anyone could object to it.
106. Hide the decision you think will attract attention.
107. Make the next move answer an imagined criticism.
108. Stop just before crossing a boundary you invented.
109. Let one failed action narrow all later possibilities.
110. Make permission uncertain and respond conservatively.
111. Reward one kind of mark and watch the others disappear.
112. Penalise something once and see whether you avoid it thereafter.
113. Work under a rule you can infer but never read.
114. Let the work teach you what not to do.

## Boundary strategies

115. Make one deliberate element look like a mistake.
116. Treat the central element as merely decorative.
117. Force one part of the work to justify its presence.
118. Turn a solution into a problem and continue from there.
119. Answer one element with another instead of removing it.
120. Keep only what survives an irrelevant criterion.

# Mining Pass 2

A deliberately looser mining pass. The academic taxonomy is now only a source of operations; fidelity to it is not a goal. The criterion is whether an operation might provoke an interesting artistic move.

## Selection, admission, preference

1. Keep only what passes an irrelevant test.
2. Let the weakest element decide what stays.
3. Remove everything that agrees too easily.
4. Choose according to a criterion you cannot defend.
5. Admit one exception and build around it.
6. Keep what almost belongs.
7. Reject the thing that solves the problem.
8. Prefer the element that arrived last.
9. Let one part earn the right to remain.
10. Keep only what would normally be discarded.
11. Select for awkwardness.
12. Choose what makes the rest more difficult.
13. Remove the most accomplished part.
14. Keep the compromise.
15. Decide what counts as finished, then violate it.

## Status, legitimacy, authority

16. Make the mistake look authoritative.
17. Treat the central element as an interruption.
18. Give the least important thing the final word.
19. Make one element justify its presence.
20. Let something trivial become the standard.
21. Treat ornament as evidence.
22. Promote a supporting element beyond its competence.
23. Make the obvious solution look suspicious.
24. Give an accident institutional authority.
25. Let the copy outrank the original.
26. Treat the correction as the error.
27. Make something look unofficial.
28. Give authority to what cannot explain itself.
29. Let confidence determine prominence.
30. Make the least reliable element impossible to ignore.

## Reversal and inversion

31. Turn the solution into the problem.
32. Protect what was meant to be temporary.
33. Treat the interruption as the work.
34. Preserve the failure and remove the attempt.
35. Make the exception govern the rule.
36. Use the frame as content.
37. Make the background responsible for the foreground.
38. Treat evidence as decoration.
39. Make the unwanted effect indispensable.
40. Let the constraint become the subject without announcing it.
41. Make the repair more visible than the damage.
42. Reverse what needs permission.

## Contradiction and counterforce

43. Answer every gesture with another gesture.
44. Let nothing occur without opposition.
45. Introduce a second explanation that is equally convincing.
46. Make two incompatible readings equally necessary.
47. Correct something until it becomes unstable.
48. Oppose the strongest element without removing it.
49. Put the answer beside its negation.
50. Make every assertion produce a counter-assertion.
51. Create an argument that neither side can win.
52. Make agreement impossible without making conflict obvious.
53. Add a response that changes the question.
54. Let one element continually interrupt another.

## Precedent, inheritance, sediment

55. Continue a rule after its reason has disappeared.
56. Treat an accident as precedent.
57. Let yesterday's solution become today's constraint.
58. Keep the correction after the mistake is gone.
59. Preserve a compromise nobody remembers making.
60. Inherit a rule from another work.
61. Continue a pattern you no longer believe in.
62. Let repetition turn a decision into a fact.
63. Keep something merely because it is already there.
64. Allow an early choice to govern everything that follows.
65. Let the first version haunt the final one.
66. Work from the consequences of a decision you never made.
67. Preserve the residue and remove its cause.
68. Let a temporary measure become permanent.
69. Make an arbitrary beginning increasingly difficult to escape.
70. Treat history as a material.

## Defaults and invisibility

71. Make the strongest rule the one nobody notices.
72. Create a default and stop choosing.
73. Repeat something until it no longer appears deliberate.
74. Hide the decision inside the procedure.
75. Make an arbitrary choice seem inevitable.
76. Let habit finish the work.
77. Put the most consequential decision somewhere unremarkable.
78. Make the convention invisible by obeying it perfectly.
79. Change one default and nothing else.
80. Notice what you have stopped noticing.
81. Let ease determine form.
82. Make the path of least resistance visible.
83. Remove the alternative rather than choosing.
84. Make one assumption impossible to question.

## Memory and archive

85. Decide what the work is allowed to remember.
86. Preserve the trace but lose the event.
87. Keep the evidence and discard the explanation.
88. Remove the earliest version.
89. Destroy the record of one decision.
90. Archive only the failures.
91. Let the documentation contradict the work.
92. Preserve what was removed.
93. Make the archive more complete than the object.
94. Forget selectively.
95. Leave evidence of something that never happened.
96. Remove the evidence that a convention was once a choice.
97. Reconstruct something from an incomplete record.
98. Work from a memory you know is wrong.
99. Let absence become the most reliable record.
100. Keep only the versions that led nowhere.

## Irreversibility and reopening

101. Make one decision impossible to undo.
102. Close a possibility earlier than necessary.
103. Reopen something you considered settled.
104. Undo the rule but keep its consequences.
105. Restore something without restoring its former role.
106. Make revision progressively more expensive.
107. Return to a decision after the work has adapted to it.
108. Reverse something too late.
109. Reintroduce what the work has learned to live without.
110. Make a permanent choice for a temporary reason.
111. Undo one foundational decision and refuse to repair the consequences.
112. Make the finished work negotiable again.

## Unwritten rules and anticipation

113. Work according to a rule you can infer but cannot state.
114. Invent a prohibition and forget you invented it.
115. Avoid something before deciding why.
116. Let an imagined audience remove one possibility.
117. Make one choice as though somebody will object.
118. Obey the rule you think exists.
119. Introduce a consequence without revealing the offence.
120. Let uncertainty narrow the work.
121. Punish one gesture once.
122. Reward one gesture until the alternatives disappear.
123. Make the work afraid of repeating itself.
124. Stop just before the thing you are most curious about.
125. Proceed as though one material were embarrassing.
126. Make a decision you would not defend publicly.
